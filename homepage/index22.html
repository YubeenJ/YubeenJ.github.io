About Me

Hi! I'm Yubeen, a 3rd-year Ph.D. student in the Department of Marine Sciences at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill. My research revolves around understanding the complex dynamics of ocean currents, with a special focus on the Gulf Stream, one of the most influential systems in the North Atlantic. I am passionate about utilizing advanced technologies, such as high-frequency radar and underwater gliders, to monitor and analyze the long-term variability of oceanic processes. Through my work, I aim to contribute to the scientific understanding of ocean-climate interactions and their implications for coastal environments and global systems.

Projects

  • Gulf Stream Variability:
    • Extract the Gulf Stream information using radial velocity map
    • Long-term monitoring of Gulf Stream position and orientation using radar data
    • Analysis of semi-annual, annual, and interannual variability in Gulf Stream speed and separation latitude
    • Exploring the relationship between Gulf Stream variability and Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC)
    • Investigating the impact of local wind patterns and sea level changes near the Charleston Bump
  • Glider Operations:
    • Optimizing waypoint navigation for gliders in dynamic ocean conditions and monitoring the Glider behaviors
    • Correction of G1 Glider's non-pumped CTD Salinity data
